Pairing fonts to the wordmark.

The Iyuno logo is a rounded geometric humanist sans — even stroke weight, generous open counters, slightly softened terminals, and a distinctively horizontal dot tittle. Below: four candidates that share its DNA, plus two editorial serifs that complement the brand's literary voice.

STROKE   even, low-contrast (monoline-ish)
TERMINALS   rounded but not soft — slightly cut
APERTURE   open (humanist, not strict geometric)
BOWLS   wide, generous, optimistic
TITTLE   horizontal ellipse — wider than tall
Y DESCENDER   curved, swooping, flat-cut tail

One brand font does the job. Switzer.

The discipline of a design system is in removing choices, not adding them. Six fonts is theatre. One sans handles every visible surface — hero, body, UI, navigation, cards. Hierarchy comes from scale and weight, not font-family swaps.

This is the same approach Stripe, Linear, and Vercel use: pick one well-drawn sans, learn it cold, ship it everywhere. The brand reads as itself at every size.

If you later need an editorial moment (a flagship report, a pull-quote-heavy long-read), add Instrument Serif as a single tactical exception. Don't bake it into the system upfront.
